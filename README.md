# Scaling pollinator camera-trap data

This repository contains the R scripts used to process pollinator camera-trap detections, fit species-specific temperature-response models, generate spatial and temporal predictions, and validate these predictions against independent GBIF occurrence records and TERENO pan-trap data.

The analysis is organised as a four-step workflow:

1. preparation of camera-trap, temperature, WorldClim, and GBIF data;
2. fitting and evaluation of species-specific temperature-response models;
3. generation of spatial predictions and validation against independent GBIF occurrence records; and
4. generation of temporal predictions and validation against independent TERENO pan-trap data.

---

### Folder contents

- `scripts/` contains the four R scripts used for the analysis.
- `raw_data/` contains the input data used by the workflow.
- `r_data/` contains intermediate R objects generated by the scripts.
- `results/` contains figures, tables, prediction rasters, model summaries, and validation outputs generated during the analysis.

## Local temperature data for the camera study

Hourly temperature data for the **Aarhus South (06074)** weather station were obtained from the Danish Meteorological Institute (DMI). The analysis uses observations from June to September 2019.

The data used in this study were downloaded on **26 August 2023** through the DMI web interface available at that time. DMI's data-access infrastructure has since changed, but the corresponding observations can still be obtained through the current DMI `metObs` API and bulk-download services.

To simplify reproduction of the workflow and to ensure that the exact files used in the analysis are available, the four CSV files are included directly in this repository. DMI Open Data are licensed under **CC BY 4.0**.

The files are located in:


## TERENO validation data

### `tereno_filtered.csv`

This file contains filtered and aggregated wild-bee monitoring data from the long-term monitoring programme of the **Terrestrial Environmental Harz/Central German Lowland Observatory (TERENO)**. (TERENO; Zacharias et al., 2011)

Wild-bee communities were monitored annually in six agricultural landscapes in Saxony-Anhalt, Germany:

- FBG – Friedeburg
- GFH – Greifenhagen
- HAR – Harsleben
- SIP – Siptenfelde
- SST – Schafstädt
- WAN – Wanzleben

Within each landscape, 16 local wild bee communities were sampled annually using standardised pan traps. Traps were emptied biweekly and operated during six weeks in spring to early summer (May–June) and six weeks in late summer (August–September). Specimens were identified to species level by experts (Frenzel et al., 2016; Papanikolaou et al., 2017).

For the analyses in this study, the original monitoring data were filtered to *Bombus terrestris* and *Bombus lapidarius* and to observations up to and including 2020. Pan-trap counts were then summed for each:

```text
site × species × sampling window × year
```

The resulting `sum_pantrap` variable represents the total number of individuals for each of these combinations.

The prepared file contains the following variables:

- `site` – TERENO landscape/site identifier
- `GenSpec` – species name
- `StartDate` – start date of the sampling interval
- `EndDate` – end date of the sampling interval
- `YearValue` – calendar year of sampling
- `sum_pantrap` – summed number of individuals for the respective site, species, sampling window, and year

With permission from the data providers, the prepared dataset required to reproduce the analyses presented in this study is included directly in this repository at:

```text
raw_data/tereno/tereno_filtered.csv
```

No additional download or file preparation is required.

The data are redistributed here specifically to facilitate reproducibility of the analyses presented in associated study. For uses beyond reproduction of the analyses presented here, please contact the authors of the source datasets (Papanikolaou et al., 2017) regarding access and permission to reuse the data.

References:

> Frenzel, M., Preiser, C., Dussl, F., Höhne, R., Nickels, V., & Creutzburg, F. (2016). TERENO (Terrestrial Environmental Observatories) wild bee monitoring in six agriculturally dominated landscapes of Saxony-Anhalt (Germany) [dataset publication series]. PANGAEA. https://doi.org/10.1594/PANGAEA.864908

> Papanikolaou, A. D., Kühn, I., Frenzel, M., & Schweiger, O. (2017). Landscape heterogeneity enhances stability of wild bee abundance under highly varying temperature, but not under highly varying precipitation. *Landscape Ecology*, 32, 581–593. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10980-016-0471-x

> Zacharias, S., Bogena, H., Samaniego, L., Mauder, M., Fuß, R., Pütz, T., Frenzel, M., et al. (2011). A network of terrestrial environmental observatories in Germany. *Vadose Zone Journal*, 10, 955–973. https://doi.org/10.2136/vzj2010.0139

### `landuse.csv`

This file contains landscape-composition and habitat-diversity metrics for 95 pan-trap sampling locations in six TERENO agricultural sites in Saxony-Anhalt, Germany.

The six TERENO sites are:

- FBG – Friedeburg
- GFH – Greifenhagen
- HAR – Harsleben
- SIP – Siptenfelde
- SST – Schafstädt
- WAN – Wanzleben

Landscape variables were calculated within a 200 m buffer around each pan-trap sampling location.

The file contains the following variables:

- `Name` – pan-trap sampling-location identifier; the first three letters identify the TERENO site
- `arable` – proportion of the 200 m buffer classified as arable land
- `forest` – proportion of the 200 m buffer classified as forest
- `managed_grassland` – proportion of the 200 m buffer classified as managed grassland
- `semi_natural` – proportion of the 200 m buffer classified as semi-natural habitat
- `urban` – proportion of the 200 m buffer classified as urban habitat
- `NA` – proportion of the 200 m buffer not assigned to one of the five aggregated habitat categories
- `crop_div` – Shannon diversity of crop types within the 200 m buffer
- `crop_rich` – number of crop types within the 200 m buffer
- `mass_fl_area` – area of mass-flowering crops within the 200 m buffer in square metres (m²)
- `mass_fl_pa` – presence (`1`) or absence (`0`) of mass-flowering crops within the 200 m buffer
- `EUNIS_div_agg` – Shannon diversity of the aggregated EUNIS habitat categories within the 200 m buffer
- `EUNIS_rich_agg` – number of aggregated EUNIS habitat categories within the 200 m buffer
- `EUNIS_div` – Shannon diversity of the original EUNIS level-3 habitat classes within the 200 m buffer
- `EUNIS_rich` – number of original EUNIS level-3 habitat classes within the 200 m buffer

With permission from the relevant data providers, the prepared dataset required to reproduce the analyses presented in this study is included directly in this repository at:

```text
raw_data/tereno/landuse.csv
```

No additional download or file preparation is required.

The data are redistributed here specifically to facilitate reproducibility of the analyses presented in this study. For uses beyond reproduction of the analyses presented here, please contact the original data providers or authors of the source publication (Slabbert. et al. 2024) regarding access and permission to reuse the data.

Reference:

> Slabbert, E., Knight, Tm., Wubet, T., Frenzel, M., Singavarapu, B., & Schweiger, O. (2024). Climate and land use primarily drive the diversity of multi-taxonomic communities in agroecosystems. *Basic and Applied Ecology*, 79, 65–73. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.baae.2024.06.003

# 4. Run the analysis

Run the scripts in numerical order:

```text
01_data_prep.R
02_activity_models.R
03_SDMs.R
04_temporal.R
```

The repository root must remain the working directory.

It is recommended to **restart R before each script** so that each analysis step starts from a clean session.

For example, the scripts can be run from the R console as follows.

### Step 1 – data preparation

```r
rm(list = ls())
source("scripts/01_data_prep.R")
```

After this script finishes, restart R.

### Step 2 – temperature–activity models

```r
rm(list = ls())
source("scripts/02_activity_models.R")
```

After this script finishes, restart R.

### Step 3 – spatial projections and GBIF evaluation

```r
rm(list = ls())
source("scripts/03_SDMs.R")
```

After this script finishes, restart R.

### Step 4 – temporal validation

```r
rm(list = ls())
source("scripts/04_temporal.R")
```

Some model-diagnostic plots are also displayed interactively while the scripts are running.

# 5. What each script does

## `01_data_prep.R`

This script prepares the principal input datasets.

It:

- imports camera-detection CSV files;
- applies the camera-detection confidence threshold;
- removes class 1 from the camera analysis;
- aggregates detections to hourly observations;
- reconstructs camera sampling effort using `recording_dates.csv`;
- joins camera observations to hourly temperature data;
- aggregates the camera data to daily abundance and daily maximum temperature;
- downloads and crops WorldClim BIO5;
- imports the GBIF occurrence download;
- applies the GBIF filtering criteria; and
- writes species-specific cleaned GBIF occurrence files.

Main intermediate files generated in `r_data/`:

```text
cam_hr.RData
temp_orig.RData
cam_day_tmax.RData
```

---

## `02_activity_models.R`

This script fits species-specific negative-binomial temperature–activity models to the daily camera-trap data.

For each focal species, it:

- accounts for daily camera effort;
- fits a quadratic temperature-response model;
- fits an intercept-only null model;
- compares the temperature model against the null model;
- checks model convergence;
- simulates model residuals;
- produces temperature-response predictions and confidence intervals; and
- generates the temperature-response figure and model-comparison table.

Outputs are written to:

```text
results/
```

---

## `03_SDMs.R`

This script projects the camera-derived temperature responses onto the WorldClim BIO5 raster.

It:

- refits the species-specific temperature–activity models;
- predicts the response across the BIO5 raster;
- aggregates the prediction rasters;
- scales predictions for visualisation;
- saves species-specific prediction rasters;
- overlays cleaned GBIF occurrences;
- calculates Boyce index values; and
- creates the multi-species spatial prediction figure.

Outputs, including GeoTIFF prediction rasters, maps, and Boyce-index tables, are written to:

```text
results/
```

---

## `04_temporal.R`

This script performs the independent temporal validation for:

- *Bombus terrestris*
- *Bombus lapidarius*

It:

- fits the camera-based temperature models for the two species;
- predicts daily activity from DWD daily maximum temperature;
- aggregates predictions across TERENO pan-trap sampling intervals;
- aggregates observations and predictions to annual values;
- fits negative-binomial validation models;
- evaluates annual temporal correspondence;
- calculates site-level temporal skill;
- compares temporal skill with landscape variables;
- clusters correlated habitat variables;
- fits the habitat-effect model; and
- generates the validation and habitat-effect figures and tables.

Additional intermediate files are written to `r_data/`, and final outputs are written to `results/`.
